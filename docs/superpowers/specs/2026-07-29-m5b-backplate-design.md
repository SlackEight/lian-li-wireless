# M5b: BACKPLATE — one lit object, one deliberate commit — RGB-only UI design
**Date:** 2026-07-29  **Status:** Proposed (awaiting owner decisions — see Open questions)
Produced by an 11-agent design workflow: 3 investigations (fan-removal audit, protocol/multi-devicefacts, unsparing UI critique) → 4 independent design directions → 3 judging lenses (owner task,technical feasibility, visual craft) → synthesis. Run `wf_65b76205-521`.

## Pitch

llw becomes a single lit object you edit directly. One always-on plate draws all five devices at their real, WASM-exact LED geometry, arranged the way they physically sit in the case, each looping its own animation on its own clock exactly as the hardware does — and it draws them as *light*, not as dots: the SL-INF's five wiring segments are stroked as continuous luminous guides with a half-resolution bloom pass, so the preview finally looks like the thing on the desk. Tools sit at the edges in solid panels that are tinted by the rig's own mean colour, so the chrome literally glows the colour of your case with zero compositing cost. Editing is free, local, and instant; committing is one white button that names its price before you press it. The design is organised entirely around the two hard truths: the ~4–5s-per-device flash commit means iteration must be free and commitment must be rare, explicit, serialized, cancellable and always undoable; and the absence of any cross-device phase lock means the preview runs each device at its own drifting offset — the app *renders* the truth instead of disclaiming it, and the word "sync" never appears as a verb. Everything the owner does five times today — the same edit, five devices, ~45s of babysitting — collapses to a default scope of "all five", one edit, one commit, one honest ~22s bar that finishes early.

## Winning base

BACKPLATE is the spine. It won owner-task (8.5) and craft (8.5) and is the only entrant that composes the rig as ONE lit object on a continuous shared ground rather than N framed widgets or a gallery of tiles — which is the single decision that separates a picture from a layout, and the owner's complaint ("aesthetic needs a lot of work", "should be an exceptionally good looking UI") is a picture complaint. It is also structurally correct about the hardware in a way the others are not: a canvas of local drafts with exactly one paid gesture is the only shape that makes a mandatory 3s-per-device flash commit livable, and a canvas rendering five independently-looping animations is *structurally incapable* of promising a lockstep sweep the firmware can't deliver. Its feasibility score (7.5, third) came from three specific, individually fixable defects rather than from the shape: (1) it conflated UI preview compilation with daemon upload compilation when specifying the common frame count — fixed here by an additive `EffectSpec.frames` field and an explicit statement that only the daemon compiles what plays; (2) it bet its entire material identity on `backdrop-filter: blur(24px)` over a continuously repainting canvas on webkit2gtk with no fallback — deleted entirely and replaced with solid panels carrying a sampled-rig-colour tint, which achieves the same "the tools glow the colour of your case" thesis at zero GPU cost and survives on any hardware; (3) it was the largest net-new build — reduced here by keeping the tested store layer intact, by landing the new renderer inside the existing StageCanvas slot before any layout work, and by cutting one of its four concepts (Sets) outright. Its craft flaws were equally local: the display tier was spent on Roll Call and empty states while the hero screen's largest type sat inside a floating panel (fixed: the look name lives ON the plate at 44px, always visible), and the case outline was un-art-directed (fixed: an authored chassis with material, and the owner's real case model as an open question).

## Information architecture

There is one screen. Navigation is object navigation, not section navigation. `SECTIONS` goes from four items (Lighting / Cooling / Devices / Health) to three nav rows plus a live rig roster.

THE RAIL (248px, 272px at ≥2000px, 68px icon rail below 1180px) — the thing the owner likes, structurally preserved: same position, same near-black, same hairline, same 16×16 / 1.5px-stroke inline SVG icons, same wordmark with its single light dot. Only the *content* changes, and it fills the ~600px of dead space that sat under four nav items.
  1. Wordmark lockup (56px). The dot is a real small radial light source, tinted by the rig's mean colour — so the brand mark is literally lit by the hardware. There is no purple anywhere in the app; the rig is the brand colour.
  2. Nav, three rows: RIG · LOOKS · SETUP.
  3. `DEVICES` roster — one 56px row per configured device: a 22px live glyph rendered by the same WASM engine from that device's *live* frames (a genuine miniature of what it is playing, not an icon), the name (inline click-to-rename, the good idiom already in Devices.tsx), the geometry line (`3 fans · 132 LEDs`), a sync dot, and a 2px left spine in that device's own current colour. Rows are the DOM mirror of canvas selection — keyboard-navigable, screen-reader-legible, arrow keys move selection, click selects on the plate and vice versa. Below the configured rows, any device seen on the air but not linked appears as a dimmed ghost row with a `link` chip.
  4. Flex spacer, hairline.
  5. Link strip (48px, pinned): `◉ link · ch 8 · master 32:e1`. Turns amber for a stale device, red for `tx_wedged` with `power-cycle the PSU`. Click opens a popover carrying the four surviving Health facts (link acquired, tx wedged, per-device frames-confirmed, last GetDev age). Health is not a destination; four facts do not earn a tab.

RIG (default, ~95% of use) — the plate plus the inspector plus the commit bar. Lighting, device identity, arrangement, naming, sync state and apply progress all live here. There is no reason to leave this screen to answer "is my rig lit the way I want".

LOOKS — a full-bleed overlay *over the live plate*, not a page. The grid of saved whole-rig Looks dims the inspector but leaves the plate visible, and hovering a Look renders it on the actual rig behind the overlay (free, draft-only). You shop for looks by watching your own case wear them. The History strip lives along the bottom of the same overlay.

SETUP — the only genuinely separate screen, and rarely visited: air inventory, link/unlink, Roll Call, Arrange, the Strimer Chase probe, master/channel detail, preview quality tier, and the daemon diagnostics that survive fan removal.

CONCEPT COUNT IS THREE, AND ONE IS AUTOMATIC. BACKPLATE shipped four stateful concepts (drafts vs live, Sets, Looks, History) and had to write the sentence "Sets are not Looks" to keep them apart — that is a manual. Sets are cut. Groups are *derived, never authored*: the plate auto-derives scope chips from what it already knows (`All`, `Fans`, `Strimers`, and one chip per occupied zone once arranged — `Top`, `Front`). The owner can rename a zone; they cannot invent a taxonomy. What remains: Draft vs Live (a state, always visible), Looks (saved whole-rig states), History (automatic, never authored).

THE PLATE'S CONTENTS, in canvas space:
  · An AUTHORED CHASSIS, not a rounded rect. Outer silhouette stroked at `--hairline` with real corner geometry; an inner side-panel face as a barely-there vertical gradient (rgba(255,255,255,0.012) → transparent) with a 1px top-edge light catch; mount rails drawn as short tick clusters at the real mount pitch. Zone labels at `--t-0` (11px, Martian Mono, 0.09em, uppercase, `--text-muted`): `TOP` `FRONT` `REAR` `BOTTOM` `PSU / SHROUD`.
  · Each fan at real `SlInf44` geometry from `led_layout_json`, at 150–180px diameter so the five-segment structure reads, drawn as CONTINUOUS LIGHT (see below) rather than as dots.
  · Multi-fan devices carry a TETHER: a hairline bracket linking the three fans of one SL-INF set, captioned once and quietly `one controller · one effect`. Clicking any fan selects the whole set. Individual-fan selection is deliberately impossible in v1, and the tether teaches why — one MAC = one EffectSpec = one flash write.
  · Strimers as flat lit RIBBONS routed to the shroud — a wide cable with real thickness whose light diffuses across the width, LEDs running along its length in wire order, captioned `132 LEDs · wire order · not yet measured` with a `Chase ›` link into the probe.
  · EMPTY MOUNTS: any declared-but-unoccupied zone renders as a dashed hairline outline. AIR GHOSTS: devices on the air but not linked render as 45%-opacity wireframes with a `link` chip. STALE DEVICES desaturate to wireframe with `last seen 42s ago`. This is the state nobody drew, and it is the one that decides whether the redesign reads as premium or as broken.

THE SIGNATURE: LEDs RENDERED AS LIGHT, NOT AS DATA. The current draw path (StageCanvas.tsx:113–145) fills two arcs per LED — a halo at 3.1× core radius plus a bright core, no shadowBlur. That was a correct webkit2gtk perf compromise in M4, and it produces a scatter plot. Real SL-INF fans are diffused light guides: continuous luminous rings and arcs. Replace with a segment-continuous renderer:
  · `led_layout_json` gains a `segments: [{start, end, closed}]` array alongside `points` (~15 lines in llw-effects-wasm; SlInf44 → inner ring 0–7 closed, outer left arc 8–17, left strip 18–25, outer right arc 26–35, right strip 36–43, repeated per fan; UniformRing → one closed segment; Strip → one open segment).
  · PASS 0 — unlit guides: stroke every segment at `w` with `rgba(255,255,255,0.055)`, round caps and joins. The geometry reads even at brightness 0, which is what stops Blackout from looking like a crashed preview.
  · PASS 1 — diffusion: on an offscreen canvas at 0.5× resolution, stroke each adjacent-LED pair as a short 2-stop-gradient segment at `w × 2.6`, `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'`, alpha 0.30. Blit back to the main canvas upscaled with `imageSmoothingEnabled = true` — the upscale *is* the blur, at a fraction of a shadowBlur's cost.
  · PASS 2 — the core: on the main canvas, stroke each adjacent-LED pair at `w` with a 2-stop linear gradient between the two LED colours, `lighter`, alpha 0.9.
  For a 132-LED SL-INF set that is ~128 short gradient strokes per pass instead of 264 arc fills — the same order of cost and a categorically different image. This is the single highest-leverage visual upgrade available in the whole project, in a product whose entire differentiator is a frame-exact preview.

QUALITY TIERS, CHOSEN BY MEASUREMENT NOT ASSUMPTION. Every candidate design bet its aesthetic on an unmeasured webkit2gtk budget and specified the degraded mode as a fallback. Inverted here: on first run the app renders 120 frames of the full rig offscreen and times it. TIER A = passes 0/1/2 + ambient wash + grain. TIER B = passes 0/2 (gradient strokes, no bloom blit) + wash. TIER C = the existing dot renderer. The chosen tier is shown in Setup and is user-overridable. The 30fps floor is enforced by a frame governor that drops the wash first, then pass 1, and never the LEDs.

RESPONSIVE — real `@media` rules, of which the codebase currently has zero. ≥2000px: rail 272, gutters 48, plate grows, chassis scales. 1180–1600: inspector 340, gutters 24. <1180px: rail collapses to a 68px icon rail with the roster as a flyout. <1000px: inspector becomes a bottom sheet; the plate keeps full width. The plate never scrolls; the inspector and the Looks overlay do.

## Multi-device model

THE MODEL. `stageStore` today is an app-wide singleton holding exactly ONE working `spec`, and `selectDevice(mac)` never touches it (stage.ts:244–248) — so switching device pills silently carries your in-progress edit onto another device and displays it as that device's state. That is the root defect. Replace with `{ scope: Scope, devices: Map<mac, { committed: EffectSpec | null, draft: EffectSpec, phase: number }> }` where `committed` is seeded on mount from `config.devices[].effect` via the existing `specFromWire` (which already exists and is already unused for this purpose), so the plate shows what the rig is ACTUALLY running the instant the app opens. Today it boots to a hard-coded blue ripple regardless — open the app while the rig runs static violet and the hero shows a blue ripple. That is the app's first credibility failure and it is a one-line fix.

SCOPE, NOT SELECTION-AND-REPEAT. Scope defaults to `All 5 devices`. Every inspector control writes only its own field to every in-scope draft. Fields that differ across the scope render as `Mixed` with a small dot; touching the control resolves it for all. So the owner's number-one chore — the identical edit performed five times, ~45s of babysitting — becomes one edit by default, and "make the Strimers purple but leave the fans alone" is: click the `Strimers` chip (or shift-click the two ribbons), change the palette, commit. Two devices dirty, ~9s, and the fans' kinds and speeds are untouched by construction because per-field writes never touch fields you didn't edit.

SELECTION GRANULARITY IS THE MAC, BECAUSE THE SPEC IS. Clicking any of the three fans in the SL-INF set selects the whole set and highlights the tether. Gestures: click = select one; shift-click = add/remove; drag on empty plate = marquee by footprint intersection; ⌘A = all; Esc = back to All scope; arrow keys walk the roster order. Per-LED and per-zone addressing IS fully available at the protocol layer (`upload_rgb` takes arbitrary per-LED frames; the SlInf44 index ranges 0–7 / 8–17+26–35 / 18–25+36–43 are already mapped), so the plate is architected for a future Zones mode — but the IPC carries one flat EffectSpec, so v1 must refuse, and the tether manages that expectation rather than an error message doing it.

GROUPS ARE DERIVED, NEVER AUTHORED. `All`, `Fans`, `Strimers` come free from geometry the daemon already reports; one chip per occupied zone appears once Arrange has run. Zones are renameable; the taxonomy is not extensible. This deletes an entire authored concept (BACKPLATE's Sets) whose only job was to shortcut a selection the app can infer.

MIXED GEOMETRY NEEDS NO USER EFFORT, WITH ONE HONEST CAVEAT. A single spec compiles natively for `Fans{3,44,SlInf44}`, `Fans{1,44,SlInf44}` and `Strip{132}` alike — the spec stores intent and each device renders it in its own shape. The one place geometry leaks is meteor, which maps the palette along the *chain* (`palette(pos)`), so on the 3-fan set the gradient spans three fans and on a 1-fan set it spans one, three times as fast. The inspector states that under the palette when meteor is selected. We do NOT offer an "across the whole rig" option, because without a phase primitive it would be a lie.

MATCHED LOOP LENGTH — the real, legal sync feature. Today the daemon derives frames from `frame_budget(leds)`, so at speed 5 the 3-fan set loops in 1400 ms while each 1-fan set loops in 1920 ms: a 37% speed difference at the same UI setting, visible immediately, and about to be blamed on the new UI. When scope covers 2+ devices the app requests a common frame count of 50, which divides all five period values exactly (6000/50=120, 4200/50=84, 3000/50=60, 2100/50=42, 1400/50=28 ms — every interval clears the 20 ms floor) and fits under the smallest budget on this rig (53, the 174-LED Strimer). The inspector then prints `matched loop · 3.00 s · all devices` instead of five different numbers. DELIVERY, stated precisely because three of four candidate designs got this wrong: the UI does not compile what the hardware plays — `rgb_tick` (supervisor.rs:668) hardcodes `effects_bridge::frame_budget(rec.total_leds())`. Matched loop therefore requires an additive `frames: Option<u16>` on `EffectSpec` (serde default None → daemon keeps today's behaviour) and a one-line change at that call site. PRICE IT: changing the frame count changes the compiled bytes, therefore the `effect_index`, therefore the day matched-loop turns on costs exactly one full re-flash of all five devices — about 22 seconds and five flash writes, once. The UI says so when you enable it. Cost thereafter is smoothness: 60 ms/frame instead of 42 at speed 3, which the inspector shows as `50 frames · 60 ms` versus `70 frames · 42 ms`.

PHASE HONESTY IS RENDERED, NOT DISCLAIMED. There is no phase field in the upload header, no trigger command, and the 1 Hz master clock carries a deliberately-zero payload; two devices sharing an `effect_index` share only an FNV-1a hash of identical frames. So each device on the plate carries its own `phase` offset and visibly drifts against the others — the preview shows exactly what the case will look like. The offset is a random value re-rolled when that device's commit confirms, because that is where the real offset comes from (whenever the flash commit landed); seeding it from the MAC, as one candidate proposed, would imply a stable knowable phase that does not exist. Copy law: the UI says `grouped`, `same look`, `matched loop`. The word "sync" never appears as a verb about devices. `rgb_in_sync` is surfaced as `frames confirmed`.

INTRA-DEVICE FAN ORDER — the gap none of the four designs could close. Every candidate arranges *devices*; none can say which of the three chained fans in one SL-INF set is physically the top one. `Geometry::Fans` carries only `{fan_count, leds_per_fan, layout}`; the WASM layout export emits `fan` as the bare loop index; and StageCanvas.tsx:109 hard-codes `cellW * (p.fan + 0.5)` — left-to-right by wire order. A daisy chain's index order almost never matches physical placement, so "the middle fan in the preview is actually my top fan" is a likely permanent complaint. The fix is cheap and purely presentational: additive config `fan_slots: Option<Vec<u8>>` (a draw-order permutation) and `mirror: Option<bool>`, applied only at draw time by mapping `p.fan` through the permutation inside `px()`. In Arrange mode, dragging one ring within its own enclosure swaps positions and a mirror toggle flips the enclosure. About 20 lines of renderer change, no protocol or effect-engine involvement.

ROLL CALL — identification and arrangement as ONE gesture, at zero marginal flash cost. Two 1-fan SL-INF sets and two Strimers are indistinguishable by name, and the only way to make a device announce itself on this hardware is a flash write — identify is never free. So: Roll Call auto-scopes to only the AMBIGUOUS devices (on this rig four, since the 3-fan set and the differing-LED Strimers are already distinguishable by geometry), assigns each a distinguishable solid colour, and commits them as ONE serialized batch — and it commits them as the *first half* of the look you were going to apply anyway, using your intended look as the restore batch, so the second write is not wasted. It states its cost up front: `about 18 seconds; each device is written twice`. Then the full-bleed card asks `Look at your case. Which one is red?` — and the answer PLACES that device on the plate. This closes the loop every candidate left open: they all designed identification and arrangement as two separate chores when the identification question already contains the arrangement answer. Ongoing, the free identify is Solo: draft brightness 0 on everything else, which costs one commit you may well have been making anyway. Hovering a roster row dims the other devices in the PREVIEW only, and is labelled `preview only` — one candidate claimed preview-dimming answers "which one is Rear?", which is a category error and is corrected here.

## Apply + latency

RULE ZERO: NOTHING APPLIES AUTOMATICALLY, EVER. No auto-apply, no debounced upload on slider drag. Brightness is baked into the frames by `×(brightness/4)` inside `render_frame`, so a dragged brightness slider would fire a full re-flash per frame of travel. Editing is free and local; committing is one gesture. Flash wear is the stack's stated #1 reliability concern and this design respects it by construction.

DIRTY COMES FROM CONFIG. `dirty(mac) = draft ≠ config.devices[mac].effect`. This is exact, it includes speed, and it survives app restart. The current UI derives dirty from an in-memory `lastApplied` map, which produces three live bugs: after applying to A, switching to B lights Apply for no reason; switching back to A leaves Apply *disabled* so you cannot re-push after an RF hiccup; and every fresh launch reads dirty on everything. Seeding `committed` from config fixes all three at once.

CONFIRMATION IS SEPARATE FROM DIRTY, AND IT HAS A NAMED BLIND SPOT. `rgb_in_sync` is `expected_fx == rec.effect_index` — an FNV-1a hash of the frame bytes. I verified in llw-effects/src/lib.rs that `render_animation` renders at `t = i/frames` and speed ONLY sets `interval_ms`; the frame bytes are identical for two specs differing only in speed, and `DeviceRecord` echoes no interval field. Therefore `effect_index` CANNOT confirm the speed component of what you applied. One candidate built its headline feature (diff-aware GO from effect_index) directly on top of this and would have reported `Already live` for a change the user can see. Here: dirty drives Apply (exact), `effect_index` drives the per-device health dot (pixels only), the badge reads `frames confirmed` not `synced`, and the inspector notes once that a speed-only change is applied but not independently verifiable from the device's report. A device that is NOT dirty but IS out of sync gets a `Re-assert` chip on its plate enclosure — which is also the fix for today's trap where an RF drift leaves you unable to re-send the same effect without perturbing the spec first.

SERIALIZATION IS FREE AND HAPPENS IN THE UI. Three of four candidate designs listed "serialise rgb_tick with a per-device settle armed from upload completion" as a hard daemon prerequisite, and framed 5s-vs-16s as a product fork for the owner to adjudicate. Both are wrong, and I verified why: `rgb_tick` (supervisor.rs:653–715) has no upload queue — dirty state IS config state, and it only batches when several devices become dirty in the same instant, which happens only because the UI fires five `SetEffect` calls at once. If the UI issues `SetEffect` for device N+1 only after device N confirms — which `applyFlow` already knows how to detect, stale-true guard and all — then serialization with a true per-device settle happens TODAY with zero Rust changes. This removes the biggest listed blocker from every candidate design, makes the batch-vs-serial choice a per-commit UI decision rather than a daemon-wide policy, and makes Cancel real: today's `dismiss()` is a UI-store concept with no daemon counterpart, so under a batch model every design's Cancel button was a lie. Under UI-driven serialization, Cancel genuinely stops the queue.

THE HONEST NUMBER, DERIVED. Every candidate quoted ~16s for five devices and drew a bar against a figure it had not derived. Per device: upload 0.08–0.23s (measured; payloads run 14 B static to 8,099 B meteor, all far under the 55,880 B ceiling — payload size is not a lever) + a 3.0s settle during which the daemon does not poll at all, because `RGB_SETTLE` gating sits in `step()` above `poll_devices` + at least one 250 ms poll + the device's own report cadence before `effect_index` echoes back. That is 4–5s per device, so 20–25s for five. The UI says `about 22s` and finishes early. A ceiling that finishes early beats a floor that overruns.

THE COMMIT CONTROL NAMES ITS PRICE BEFORE THE PRESS. `APPLY — 2 devices · about 9s`. With nothing dirty it reads `nothing to send` and is inert but not dead — `Re-assert all` is one keystroke away. Hovering a Look in the overlay shows the same figure before you even load it: `→ 3 devices · about 13s`. The user learns that most changes are cheap, which is the direct antidote to the app's defining complaint.

THE QUEUE IS REAL INSTRUMENTATION. On commit the bar expands to ~120px into a per-device timeline, ordered by visual prominence — the 3-fan set first, then the Strimers, then the single fans — so the rig visibly changes within the first ~5s even though the queue runs 22. Each chip runs three truthfully-named segments: `upload` (a flash, not a bar — it is 200 ms), `radio silence` (a 3.000s LINEAR countdown ring, labelled `holding the radio quiet so the device can write to flash`), and `confirm` (indeterminate until `rgb_in_sync` resolves). Queued chips read `starts in 6.2s`. The header reads `2 of 5 · about 13s left` in tabular numerals. Naming the mechanic converts a hang into visible care.

NOTHING LOCKS. Today `busy` disables the kind list, every swatch and all three segmented controls for the entire ~9s, then a further 2.5s after success because `phase === 'done'` sits in the disabled expression. Here the inspector is never disabled. Only the in-flight device's own plate enclosure shows its state; you keep editing, and edits to a device mid-flight simply queue behind it — the existing per-mac `seq` orphaning already supports exactly this.

OPTIMISM WITH A SNAP-BACK. While a device is committing, the plate renders the target frames with a faint shimmer. On confirm the shimmer clears and the device PULSES ITS OWN LIGHT ONCE over 400 ms. Five confirmations across 22 seconds read as the rig coming up in sequence, like a theatre cue — the slowest thing in the product becomes the most beautiful thing in it. On failure the device snaps back to its last confirmed frames within 200 ms, the chip turns amber with the daemon's refusal verbatim, and the marker PERSISTS on the enclosure and the roster row until dismissed, with `Retry`. No four-second toast being the only trace of a failure.

WRITES THAT MUST NOT COST FLASH. `apply_config` calls `build_runtimes`, which resets `expected_fx: None` for ALL devices, and `needs` is unconditionally true when `expected_fx` is None — so today any `SetConfig`, including renaming a device or saving a preset, re-flashes all five devices on the next poll. Looks, History, names, arrangement and `fan_slots` all live in config. Required daemon fix: `apply_config` must preserve `expected_fx` and `last_rgb_upload` for devices whose effect is byte-identical. Same fix makes boot free: `effect_index_from_frames` is a pure FNV-1a over frames which are a pure function of (spec, geometry, frame count), so the daemon can compute the expected index at startup and skip the upload entirely — today every boot costs five flash writes and a settle storm, which reads to the user as "the app is slow at launch". Roughly 15 lines, and it is a prerequisite for the Looks feature to be usable at all.

BLACKOUT AND NIGHT. Blackout (`⌘⇧B`) drafts brightness 0 everywhere — the only clean off the hardware has (it produces genuinely all-black frames) — and focuses Apply. NIGHT is a pinned first-class Look: brightness 1, every other field preserved from the current live look, bound to `⌘⇧N`. Every candidate correctly identified that brightness is baked into the frames and must never be a scrub; not one then noticed that "it's too bright, dim it for tonight" is the single most frequent adjustment an RGB owner makes, and that in all four designs it costs a full ~22s whole-rig re-flash reached through the general editing path. Night makes it one keystroke with its cost stated once.

THE 15-SECOND PATH. Cold start must not require the maximalist canvas to warm up. First paint (<150 ms) draws the chassis, the roster, the title and the unlit guides FROM CONFIG ALONE — no WASM required; frames stream in after and ignite. A Tauri global shortcut raises the window into the last-edited scope. `⌘1..9` loads a Look as draft; `Enter` commits — two keys from cold. A tray menu commits Blackout / Night / the last three Looks without opening the window at all. Task 6, the highest-frequency task by an order of magnitude, was the one every candidate answered with "boot the hero canvas and navigate".

## Looks, history, effect discovery

LOOKS ARE WHOLE-RIG, AND THEY ARE THE ONLY SAVED OBJECT. Today a preset is `{name, effect}` — one spec, no device binding — and clicking one explicitly does not apply; the title attribute literally reads `load preset (does not apply)`. That solves nobody's problem. A Look is `{ id, name, created_at, updated_at, devices: [{ mac, slot, kind, spec }] }`. `Config.presets` is documented as opaque daemon pass-through — stored, round-tripped, never interpreted — so the shape is free to change with zero protocol work beyond the `expected_fx` fix.

DEGRADE GRACEFULLY, NEVER BLANK. `slot` and `kind` are carried so a Look survives a rebind or a replacement: an unmatched MAC falls back to slot+kind with a visible `matched by position` note, and any device the Look cannot match is LEFT ALONE, never blanked.

THE GALLERY IS AN OVERLAY ON THE LIVE PLATE. Cards are 220×132, each showing a still of the rig rendered from that Look (same engine, its peak-luminance frame, cached as a data URL) plus a colour bar of its palettes and a device count. HOVERING A CARD RENDERS THE LOOK ACROSS THE REAL PLATE BEHIND THE OVERLAY — free, draft-only, instantly reversible. You shop for looks by watching your own case wear them, and you never wait to find out whether you like something. Click loads it as draft for every device it targets, the overlay closes, and the commit bar reads `5 devices changed · about 22s`. A Look is never applied silently: loading and committing stay separate, which is today's preset behaviour promoted from a footnote to a feature. Right-click → `Apply to scope only` puts three of five devices on a Look and leaves the rest.

HISTORY IS AUTOMATIC AND IT HAS A STATED HOME. Every commit archives the outgoing live state — with its poster frame — into a rolling 10-entry strip along the bottom of the Looks overlay, timestamped in plain language (`12 min ago`, `yesterday 21:04`). One click loads it as a draft. You cannot lose a look you liked, because committing over it saves it. Three of the four candidate designs promised timestamped auto-snapshots and never said where they live, and the fourth had none at all; History must survive an app restart, so it is written to daemon config under an opaque `history` key — which is exactly why the `apply_config` / `expected_fx` fix is a hard prerequisite rather than an optimisation. Saving a Look or a History entry must cost ZERO flash writes. Non-negotiable.

THREE INDEPENDENT UNDO PATHS, NONE OF WHICH TOUCHES HARDWARE UNTIL YOU SAY SO: `Revert to live` in the commit bar (instant, free); ⌘Z / ⌘⇧Z over the draft graph (unlimited within a session, free, because drafts are local, with commit as the checkpoint); and History (persistent, one click to draft). Plus hold-to-compare: holding `\` or press-and-holding the LIVE/DRAFT chip cross-dissolves THE WHOLE RIG to its committed state in 180 ms for as long as you hold, then returns. That is A/B comparison at zero cost and zero risk, and it is the complete answer to "fear of breaking a good setup".

FIRST RUN IS SEEDED FROM THE OWNER'S OWN RIG. Migration reads the live config and creates a Look called `Current` capturing exactly what is there — static #8800FF on the three SL-INF sets, static [#8800FF, #A51D2D] on one Strimer, meteor [#8800FF, white] on the other. The owner opens the app and immediately sees their real rig, correctly modelled, including the fact that two devices already differ. The whole model is taught on their own hardware, for free, in one screen. Six starter Looks ship so the shelf is never empty, each authored per-geometry because meteor on a wire-order ribbon is not the same object as meteor on a fan chain: `Deep Violet` (#8800FF static — the owner's existing taste, so the app opens familiar), `Ember`, `Ice`, `Aurora` (colour-cycle, matched loop), `Chase` (meteor on chains, runway on ribbons), and the two pinned system Looks `Night` (brightness 1) and `Blackout` (brightness 0), which are undeletable.

EFFECT DISCOVERY — EIGHT LIVE THUMBNAILS, NOT EIGHT WORDS. The current chooser is eight buttons whose entire content is `prettyKind(kind)`; nothing distinguishes `Colour cycle` from `Rainbow morph` until you click and burn a commit. The M4 spec promised thumbnails and the UI never delivered them. The grid renders each kind by the same WASM engine on a cheap miniature geometry, IN THE CURRENT PALETTE, so you preview your colours. HOVER = TRY-ON: hovering a tile renders that kind across every in-scope device on the real plate, live, at real geometry, for free; move away and it snaps back. You discover effects by scrubbing the grid and watching your own case. Each tile carries `EffectKind::describe()` — which exists in llw-effects and the UI imports neither it nor `directional()`.

CONTROLS STOP LYING, AND THEY GO DARK IN PLACE RATHER THAN DISAPPEARING (the `unlit = inactive` grammar, so the panel never reflows and muscle memory holds). Palette semantics are per kind and two kinds ignore the palette entirely: `static` lights slot 1 only (`one colour`); `runway` lights slots 1–2 relabelled `ON` / `OFF` and darkens 3–8 (entries 3–8 are genuinely discarded by the renderer); `rainbow` and `rainbow-morph` darken ALL eight with the caption `built-in spectrum — palette not used` (both render functions literally take `_spec`); `breathing`, `colour-cycle` and `ripple` light all eight and are drawn as a TIMELINE with a moving playhead, because `palette(t)` sweeps over time and the whole device is one colour per frame; `meteor` lights all eight and is drawn as a GRADIENT BAR with draggable stops, because `palette(pos)` maps along the chain and it is the only kind where a multi-colour palette is a spatial gradient — which is also why reordering swatches is semantically real here. The direction control is dark for the five non-directional kinds; today it is enabled for all eight, so toggling it on Static marks the spec dirty and burns a full commit for a guaranteed no-op.

THE COLOUR PICKER IS THE PRODUCT, SO IT GETS BUILT PROPERLY. Today each swatch programmatically clicks a hidden `<input type="color">`, which on the owner's KDE/webkit2gtk build pops the GTK chooser — a bright, out-of-theme OS modal over a #0b0b0f app, and the single most jarring moment in the product. Deleted. In its place, an in-theme popover on `--surface-3`: hue rail + SV square, hex field in mono, drag-to-reorder swatches, always-visible 20px delete targets (not 14px hover-only), a recents row, a persistent 12-slot rig palette shared across Looks, and one-click harmonies (complement / triad / analogous / mono ramp) seeded from the active swatch. Swatches are 36px, not 26px — the device's colour should be one of the loudest things in the panel.

EYEDROPPER FROM THE PLATE. Press `I`, click any point on the plate, and its RENDERED colour lands in the active slot; the cursor becomes a magnified loupe. The frames are already in memory so it costs nothing, and it is the only sane way to match a Strimer to the exact violet a fan is showing at frame 23.

PER-DEVICE HONESTY NOTES, shown contextually rather than as a permanent disclaimer: with a Strimer in scope and a sweep effect selected — `sweeps in wire order · physical position on Strimers has not been measured · Chase ›`. With a 3-fan set — `one controller drives all three fans; chain effects sweep across the set, ring effects repeat per fan`. With 2+ devices — `same look, matched 3.00 s loop, independent phase — they will not sweep across the case in step`.

THE STRIMER CHASE PROBE, shipped rather than deferred. Every candidate drew a confident ribbon and captioned it `wire order`; only one proposed to measure it, and none scoped the measurement. Concretely: the LED counts factor into exactly two families with a shared per-strand length — 116 = 4×29 and 174 = 6×29; 88 = 8×11 and 132 = 12×11 — which is strong arithmetic support for a strands × positions ribbon rather than a single run, and it means sweep effects may read as strands lighting in turn rather than travelling down the cable. One upload answers it: at the 174-LED budget of 53 frames, send a single white band ~1/8 of the buffer wide sweeping once through wire order on black. The owner watches it once (~5 s, one flash write) and answers a three-option card: *travels smoothly end to end* / *jumps between parallel strands* / *something else — record a clip*. A confirmed strand-major result promotes `Strip` to a measured `StrimerRibbon` layout exactly as SlInf44 was derived on 2026-07-14, and every preview in the app improves at once.

## Aesthetic specification

NOCTURNE CHARGED, EVOLVED ALONG ONE AXIS: THE CHROME IS LIT BY THE ARTWORK, AND NOTHING ELSE IS SATURATED.

THE ACCENT LAW, RESTATED AS LAW. The current implementation inverts its own thesis: a single 15%-alpha purple is the selected-state fill for six unrelated control types (sidebar nav, stage pills, effect rows, every segmented control, curve rows, the Apply button) while the devices' actual RGB is confined to 26px swatches. The new law has three clauses and no exceptions. (1) NOTHING IN THE CHROME IS SATURATED — the purple token is DELETED outright, including from the wordmark; the dot is instead a real small light source tinted by the rig's mean colour, so the brand mark is lit by the hardware. (2) SELECTION IS THE OBJECT'S OWN LIGHT in the rail and the Looks deck (a 2px left spine and a live glyph in that object's current colour), and NEUTRAL on the plate (a 1px `rgba(255,255,255,0.55)` ring with 2px corner brackets, offset 8px outside the footprint) because colour there would fight the content. Everywhere else, "active" is elevation plus a brighter hairline plus full-strength text. (3) THE COMMIT CONTROL IS THE ONLY PURE-WHITE FILLED SURFACE IN THE APPLICATION — solid `#ffffff` with `#0b0b0f` ink. This is a deliberate correction to every candidate design: they all reserved a purple for commit, and the owner's live rig runs #8800FF, so the one saturated element in the app would sit in the same hue family as the content behind it and camouflage. Commit is carried by value and fill, never by hue, so it is unmistakable against any RGB the rig can produce.

COLOUR TOKENS — add / change:

User-facing failure text never contains npm commands.

Ship two self-hosted OFL variable faces, latin-subset woff2: **Archivo Variable** for UI, with **Archivo Expanded 700** at the display tier — an expanded grotesque reads like a lighting desk and a broadcast graphic, which is exactly what this product is — and **Martian Mono** for MACs, hex, frame counters, LED counts and micro-labels, an instrument-panel mono with real character (JetBrains Mono is the safe substitute if Martian reads too wide in situ). The current `--font: -apple-system, 'Segoe UI', system-ui` resolves to neither of its first two choices on Arch/KDE, so today's typography is whatever the distro decided.

SPACING — extend `4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24` upward to `4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96` and use the top of the scale deliberately. Plate gutter 32 (48 at ≥2000px). Inspector padding 20, section gaps 28. Commit bar 76. Roster row 56 with 8 gap. Inside control clusters, 8–12. The current stylesheet's largest spacing primitive is also its page padding, and every section is a flat 16px stack — which is precisely why nothing reads as more important than anything else. In a low-chrome dark language, hierarchy is almost entirely scale and space.

RADII — plate 22, panel 16, control 10, chip 999, roster row 12.

MATERIAL — three layers, no compositing tricks.
  · ELEVATION: exactly three — L0 `--plate` (the void), L1 `--surface` + hairline (rail, inspector, commit bar), L2 `--surface-3` + `--hairline-strong` + `inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.05)` (popovers, picker, expanded rows). That inset top light-catch is the one material trick and it costs nothing.
  · PANEL TINT — this replaces `backdrop-filter` entirely. BACKPLATE bet its material identity ("the tools glow the colour of your case") on `blur(24px) saturate(1.2)` over a continuously repainting canvas, which on webkit2gtk forces a backdrop readback and blur every frame and is the first thing perf would cut, leaving three flat grey panels and no identity. Instead: panels are SOLID, and each carries (a) a 1px top edge in `color-mix(--hairline-strong 65%, --rig-mean 35%)` and (b) an inner `linear-gradient(rgba(--rig-mean, 0.05), transparent 140px)`. Same payoff, zero GPU cost, survives every quality tier, and legibility is never at the mercy of what the rig is playing.
  · AMBIENT WASH — one 64×36 offscreen canvas. Each frame, paint one soft radial per device at that device's plate position in that device's mean colour; draw the buffer stretched full-window behind everything with `filter: blur(40px)` at 6% opacity. A purple Strimer on the shroud washes the bottom of the window. It is the design language's bloom promoted from decoration to architecture, and it is what stops near-black from reading as an empty div. The same tiny buffer is the source for `--rig-mean`.
  · GRAIN — 1.2% procedural noise from an inline SVG `feTurbulence`, fixed, full-window, no external asset. Kills banding on soft radials over #0b0b0f and gives the near-black a photographic texture.
  · LED LIGHT — the segment-continuous renderer specified in the primary screen. This is the aesthetic centrepiece: the difference between a scatter plot and a lit object.

MOTION — one curve, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`. Four durations: 140 ms (control state), 220 ms (enter/exit), 320 ms (layout, row expansion), 400 ms (confirm bloom). Selection brackets scale 1.04 → 1 over 120 ms. Hold-to-compare cross-dissolves the whole rig in 180 ms. THE SOLE LINEAR EXCEPTION is the 3.000 s radio-silence countdown, because it represents a real physical duration and easing it would misrepresent it.

MOTION AT REST — the gap none of the four addressed. All four specified transitions and a hero moment; none described what the app feels like CONTINUOUSLY while the rig loops at 3.00 s forever. Here the chrome is coupled to the hardware's actual period: the hairline under the title lockup breathes 0.06 → 0.12 alpha once per loop, and a 1px highlight in `--rig-mean` at 12% alpha traverses the plate's bottom edge once per loop. Two nearly-subliminal elements, both driven by the same clock the devices are on, and together they make the app feel alive WITH the hardware rather than being a still frame with a video playing inside it.

REDUCED MOTION — `prefers-reduced-motion` (currently unhandled, in an app that animates canvases continuously) zeroes all chrome transitions, stops the period sweep and the breathing hairline, holds the ambient wash static, and freezes every preview to a representative frame with a visible play toggle. The rig animation is content, so it is offered rather than removed.

THE HERO MOMENT — first paint. 0–150 ms: chassis silhouette, roster, title and unlit guides render from CONFIG ALONE, no WASM required — so the app is correct and composed before the engine loads. 150–500 ms: the chassis line completes, blueprint-style. 500–900 ms: the rig ignites — every device's real frames start playing, the ambient wash blooms across the plate, the title's hairline takes its first breath. Subsequent launches run a 350 ms short version. Two secondary beats: holding `\` (the whole rig cross-dissolves between draft and live — seeing your case change and change back is the moment this reads as a real instrument), and the commit sequence (a white sweep crosses the commit bar, five linear countdown rings begin their exact three seconds, and each device pulses its own light as it lands).

THE APP AT REST, UNLIT, AND BLACKED OUT — the state every candidate skipped, and a large share of real pixels. Under Blackout the frames are genuinely all black, so the plate would collapse to an empty rectangle indistinguishable from a crashed preview. Here the unlit guides (pass 0, `rgba(255,255,255,0.055)` continuous strokes) hold the entire rig's geometry as dark glass; the chassis keeps its edge light-catch; the ambient wash fades to zero over 900 ms and the panel tints go neutral; the title reads `BLACKOUT` at `--t-6` with `lights out · 5 devices` beneath. The app looks like an object sitting in a dark room, which is a state, not a failure.

ONE AUTHORED ASSET. Four near-black interfaces built entirely from hairlines, dots and text is why none of the candidates would produce a screenshot. Two things get drawn by hand here: the CHASSIS (a real silhouette with corner geometry, a side-panel glass face, mount-rail tick clusters, and a top-edge light catch — not a rounded rect with uppercase labels) and the WORDMARK LOCKUP (`llw` set in Archivo Expanded with the dot as an actual radial light source tinted by the rig). Both are small, both are the difference between "software" and "a thing someone made".

EARN THE SECOND MONITOR. A detachable RIG VIEW — a second Tauri window, borderless, full-bleed plate, no chrome, look name at `--t-7`, ambient wash edge to edge, live and non-interactive except hold-to-compare. This is the daily-joy, screenshot-generating move, and it is the only proposal here that uses the owner's actual two-monitor desktop for anything other than more layout.

## Risks

"RENDER COST IS THE PRIMARY BET, AND IT IS DE-RISKED BY SEQUENCING RATHER THAN BY OPTIMISM. Roughly 400 LEDs across five independent frame buffers, drawn as ~380 gradient strokes per pass across two passes, plus a half-resolution blur blit, plus a 64×36 ambient wash, plus a full-window grain overlay, on webkit2gtk. Every candidate design carried a version of this bet and every one specified the degraded mode as a fallback rather than as a measured default. Here the renderer ships in milestone M5b-1 at the existing 280px canvas size, BEFORE any composition work is committed to, with a first-run benchmark that picks the tier. If Tier A does not hold 30 fps on the owner's box the design still works at Tier B (gradient strokes, no bloom blit) — which is already a categorically better image than today's dots — and the loss is a softness, not an identity. That is the difference between this and BACKPLATE's original backdrop-filter bet, which had no survivable degraded form at all.\n\nTHE CHASSIS IS A DRAWING, AND A CONFIDENT DRAWING OF A WRONG MODEL IS WORSE THAN A PLAIN ONE. Even with the owner's real case model, the plate cannot represent cable routing, fan orientation, radiator thickness or airflow, and people will read adjacency into it that is not there. Two mitigations and one residual: zone labels stay generic, and the chassis is stroked as a silhouette rather than rendered as a solid object, so it reads as a map rather than as a photograph. The residual risk is real and is the price of the design's central asset.\n\nSTRIMERS ARE DRAWN AUTHORITATIVELY BEFORE THEY ARE MEASURED. The ribbon is buffer-correct and its ORDERING claim is true (wire order is wire order), but if the 4×29 / 6×29 / 8×11 / 12×11 strand factoring holds, then a sweep will read as strands lighting one after another rather than travelling down the cable — and the preview will faithfully reproduce the wrong-looking thing while looking completely sure of itself. The Chase probe (M5c-2) is scheduled precisely because captioning is not a fix, but it sits late in the sequence, so for several milestones 40% of the owner's devices are drawn with more confidence than the evidence supports.\n\nTHE PLATE INVITES AN EDIT IT CANNOT PERFORM. Drawing every LED at its true segment position — inner ring, outer arcs, side strips, all individually mapped — practically begs for 'let me paint just the inner ring'. The protocol fully supports it (`upload_rgb` takes arbitrary per-LED frames; the SlInf44 index ranges are already mapped) but the IPC carries one flat EffectSpec, so v1 must refuse. The tether teaches the atomicity, but it manages the expectation rather than eliminating the disappointment — and the better the renderer gets, the sharper that disappointment becomes. Per-zone painting is the obvious v2 and the one capability L-Connect does not have; it needs a layered IPC payload and it is a genuine scope fork, not a polish item.\n\nUI-DRIVEN SERIALIZATION IS CORRECT BUT NOT AIRTIGHT. It gives a true per-device settle with zero Rust changes, which is the single most valuable correction in this whole synthesis — but it holds only while the UI is the sole writer. A CLI `set-effect` issued during a queued commit, or a daemon drift re-assert firing mid-queue, will still put RF traffic inside someone's flash-commit window. The daemon's own `RGB_REUPLOAD_COOLDOWN` and settle gating limit the damage, and the failure mode is a retry round rather than corruption, but the guarantee is a UI convention rather than an invariant. If it proves insufficient in practice, the daemon-side per-device settle remains available as a later hardening — the UI queue and its progress instrumentation work unchanged either way.\n\nCONFIRMATION CANNOT SEE SPEED, AND THE UI SAYS SO — WHICH IS STILL A COMPROMISE. `effect_index` is an FNV-1a over frame bytes, and frames are rendered at `t = i/frames` with speed affecting only `interval_ms`, so a speed-only change is hash-identical and no device report echoes the interval. Dirty comes from config so Apply is exact, and the badge honestly reads 'frames confirmed' — but a user who changes only the speed gets a green tick that did not verify the thing they changed. Closing it properly needs the daemon to expose its last-uploaded interval per device, which it loses on restart. I judged the honest label cheaper than the new state.\n\nTHE COST ESTIMATE IS A CEILING DERIVED FROM MECHANISM, NOT FROM MEASUREMENT ON FIVE DEVICES. Per device: 0.08–0.23s upload + a 3.0s settle during which the daemon does not poll at all + at least one 250 ms poll + the device's report cadence. That is 4–5s, so 20–25s for five, and the UI says 'about 22s' and finishes early. It has not been measured end to end on the owner's actual five-device rig, and the two Strimers in particular have a history of being slow to switch that has never been fully explained. The first honest instrumented commit is itself a diagnostic, and the number in the UI should be recalibrated from it.\n\nMATCHED LOOP COSTS A REAL RE-FLASH THE DAY IT SHIPS, AND SMOOTHNESS FOREVER AFTER. Changing the frame count changes the compiled bytes and therefore the effect index: five flash writes and ~22 seconds, once, plus 17 fps instead of 24 on the SL-INFs permanently. It fixes a defect the owner can see today (37% speed mismatch at speed 5) but it is a trade, it is priced, and it is the one design decision I have deliberately left as a fork.\n\nSETUP WORK IS STILL ASSUMED, JUST LESS OF IT. Roll Call folds identification into a commit that was happening anyway and places devices from its own answer, which removes the largest chore — but a user who skips both Roll Call and Arrange gets an auto-laid-out rig, at which point the spatial premise degrades to a tidy arrangement and much of the payoff evaporates. Better than the four candidates, which each assumed two separate manual chores; not eliminated.\n\nACCESSIBILITY IS STRUCTURALLY COMPROMISED, AS IT IS FOR ANY CANVAS-FIRST TOOL. The roster mirrors every selection and every commit state in real DOM, all state changes are announced via aria-live, and ⌘K reaches everything by name — but canvas-native marquee selection, drag-arrange and the eyedropper have no keyboard or screen-reader equivalent at all. A screen-reader user gets a working list-based lighting app, not the console.\n\nIT CARRIES ZERO NON-RGB DOMAIN, BY DESIGN AND IRREVERSIBLY. With fans gone the one-plate premise has nowhere to put thermals, curves or any future non-lighting concern without breaking. That is the correct call for the stated pivot — upstream lian-li-linux keeps that domain — but it is a one-way door, and the diagnostics story is deliberately thin: the daemon still swallows GetDev `NoResponse` silently and never ages out `last_record`, so the staleness badge on the plate is only as good as evidence the daemon does not currently collect. Repointing the existing tiered `Reliability` machine at RGB/link evidence — roughly 40 lines, reusing DropoutFilter's two-poll debounce shape with `commanded → link.is_some()` and `readback_zero → absent_or_no_response` — is the honest companion fix, and it is not in this design's build sequence."

## Grafts from the runner-up directions
- LIGHT DESK — scope defaults to the whole rig, and selection narrows it. BACKPLATE's inspector only existed when something was selected, so a cold start opened to a canvas with no controls and required a discovery click. Light Desk's base-plus-overrides model fixes this: the inspector is always present and scoped to `All 5 devices` by default, which is also the owner's single most common intent. Adopted structurally, not as inheritance — see the concept cut below.
- LIGHT DESK — selection is expressed in the selected object's own light: a 2px left spine on each roster row coloured by that device's current frame, plus a live 20px glyph rendered from its real frames. This is the only proposal that puts the RGB *into* the chrome rather than merely banning purple from it, and a 248px vertical list where every row glows its own colour is genuinely beautiful and legible at a glance. Applied to the rail and the Looks deck; the plate itself keeps neutral white selection brackets, because colour there would fight the content.
- LIGHT DESK — diff-aware commit that prints its own price before you press it (`APPLY — 2 devices · ~9s`, or a disabled `nothing to send`). - LIGHT DESK — the 1.5% procedural SVG feTurbulence grain over near-black. The only entrant that noticed gradient banding on #0b0b0f, which is a real artefact on OLED and on any 8-bit panel once you add soft radial blooms. Kept, at 1.2%, as a single fixed full-window overlay.
- NOCTURNE DESK — 'unlit = inactive' as the universal control grammar. Controls that don't apply to the current kind go dark *in place* rather than disappearing, so the layout never reflows and muscle memory holds: rainbow greys all eight colour slots with `built-in spectrum — palette not used`, runway relabels slots 1–2 as ON/OFF and darkens 3–8, the direction control goes dark for the five non-directional kinds. Beats both hiding (BACKPLATE) and panel replacement (Light Desk) on layout stability, and in an RGB app the interface being itself a light fixture is thematically perfect.

Combined with BACKPLATE's scoped, cost-stated Roll Call and pushed further to close the loop the judges flagged: the owner's answer to 'which one is red?' *places* that device on the plate. Identification and arrangement stop being two chores.
- NOCTURNE DESK — 'the stage advances frames at the real interval_ms, not smoothed to 60fps, so the preview carries the same slight steppiness the hardware has. Fidelity as a flex.' The existing accumulator-based StageCanvas already does this; recognising it as a feature and *saying so in the UI* (`70 frames · 42 ms · 3.00 s loop`) is the most honest sentence any entrant wrote.
- LOOKBOOK — the ambient wash: one 64×36 offscreen canvas, upscaled and CSS-blurred, painting a low-amplitude radial sampled from the rig's real frames behind the entire window. Cheapest and most convincing 'the room glows the colour of your rig' implementation in the set. Improved here: the wash is painted per-device at each device's plate position (so a purple Strimer on the shroud washes the bottom of the window), and the same tiny buffer is sampled to tint the solid panels — which is what lets me delete backdrop-filter entirely.
- LOOKBOOK — deliberate phase desync in the preview: the app SHOWS that devices don't march in step rather than disclaiming it. Corrected: Lookbook seeded the offset from the MAC, implying a stable knowable phase. The real offset is set by whenever each flash commit landed, so it is arbitrary and re-rolls on every apply. Here each device carries a random offset that is re-rolled when that device's apply confirms — stable frame-to-frame, honest about its origin.
- LOOKBOOK — Calibrate: a per-LED chase probe so the unverified Strimer wire-order hypothesis (116=4×29, 174=6×29, 88=8×11, 132=12×11) can be *measured* rather than captioned. The only design that proposed to fix a mapping instead of apologising for it. Promoted here from a nice-to-have to a shipped tool with a concrete single-upload design.

## Open questions for the owner
- PHYSICAL ARRANGEMENT — where does each of the five devices actually sit? Specifically: which of the three SL-INF sets is the 3-fan set mounted on (top / front / side bracket), where are the two single fans (rear? bottom?), and where do the two Strimers run (24-pin down the back of the motherboard tray, GPU cables across the shroud)? And within the 3-fan chained set, which physical position is fan index 0 — the wire order almost never matches the mounting order, and getting it wrong means the preview's middle ring is your top fan forever. You can either tell me now and I seed the plate directly, or skip it and Roll Call will discover it in one ~18s commit that costs no extra flash writes.
- THE CASE — what chassis is this? I want to draw your actual case silhouette rather than a generic rounded rectangle. It is the difference between the plate reading as a picture of your rig and reading as a network diagram, and it is the one authored asset in the whole design. If you would rather it stayed abstract (a neutral dark field with zone labels and no chassis at all), say so — that is a legitimate and arguably more timeless choice, just a much quieter one.
- THE VIEWPOINT — should the plate be a FRONT ELEVATION (looking straight at the open side panel, the way you actually see your rig) or a PLAN/ISOMETRIC (looking down into the case, which shows top-mount, bottom-mount and shroud relationships more truthfully)? Elevation is more emotionally recognisable and better for the Strimers; plan is more spatially accurate for a 3-fan top radiator. This decides the hero image and it is pure taste — I have no basis to pick for you.
- THE SIXTH FAN — you expect six fan positions and the daemon reports five (a 3-fan set plus two 1-fan sets). Is the sixth fan physically installed, and if so is it chained to one of the existing controllers (in which case the controller is under-reporting its fan_count) or on a controller that has never enumerated? I need to know whether the plate should render an empty mount waiting for it, or whether five is simply correct and I should stop drawing a hole.
- TYPEFACE VOICE — I have specified Archivo Expanded 700 for the display tier and Martian Mono for numerics/labels: a broadcast-console voice with real character. The safe alternative is a single quiet family (Inter or Geist) throughout, which every competing tool uses and which will never look wrong but will also never look like anything. This is the biggest single lever on whether the app reads as 'exceptionally good looking' versus 'clean', and it is entirely your call. I can mock both against your actual rig colours if you want to see them side by side.
- MATCHED LOOP — on or off by default? ON means every device's animation loops for exactly the same duration at every speed, so the relative offset between your fans and Strimers stays constant forever instead of drifting a full period every four minutes (and it fixes a real defect: at speed 5 today your 3-fan set runs 37% faster than your single fans at the same UI setting). The cost is smoothness — 50 frames at 60 ms per frame instead of 70 at 42 ms, so ~17 fps instead of ~24 on the SL-INFs — and switching it on costs one full re-flash of all five devices, about 22 seconds, once. I recommend ON, but it is a perceptual trade you can judge in five seconds and I cannot.
- THE SECOND MONITOR — do you want a detachable full-screen Rig View window (plate only, no chrome, live, non-interactive) to leave open on your second display? It is a self-contained piece of work I would sequence late, and it is either the best thing in the app or something you close immediately and never open again. Only you know which.

## Build sequence
1. M5a — STRIP FAN CONTROL (already scoped). ~1,127 lines of daemon Rust across five whole modules, ~530 lines of supervisor surgery, ~1,400 lines of UI TS/TSX and ~530 lines of CSS delete. Config schema v2 with an in-`load` v1→v2 migration writing `config.json.v1.bak`. CRITICAL KEEP: `DeviceStatus.fan_count` and `AirDeviceStatus.fan_count` — they are RGB GEOMETRY inputs consumed by `effects_bridge::geometry_of`, not cooling fields, and deleting them silently breaks every SL-INF preview and upload. Also keep `pwm_frame` / `apply_pwm_constraints` in llw-protocol: `bind_frame` delegates to `pwm_frame` and the bind flow carries the device's current PWM through handover. Ships the free win of deleting ~10 zero-PWM RF frames per second currently aimed at the two Strimers on the same channel the RGB uploads compete for.
2. M5b-0 — PREREQUISITES, NO UI CHANGE. Four small, independently verifiable changes that every later milestone depends on. (a) `geometry: Geometry | null` and `leds: u16` on `DeviceStatus` / `AirDeviceStatus`, sourced from `effects_bridge::geometry_of(rec.kind, rec.fan_count)` and `rec.total_leds()` — additive, envelope-v1-safe by the existing `speed_source`/`curves` precedent, and it deletes stage.ts's hand-maintained FAN_KINDS/STRIP_KINDS table that is documented as deliberately diverging from the daemon. (b) `render_animation_bytes(spec, geometry, frames) -> Uint8Array` in llw-effects-wasm: the crate documents its boundary as COLD, one call per change, returning rgb as 'plain JSON numbers — no base64', which is ~100 KB of JSON string per SL-INF render — every candidate design's live-thumbnail premise violates that, so this is a hard gate, and it is ~25 lines against a crate that already has a native-testable `*_impl` split and golden parity tests. (c) `segments: [{start,end,closed}]` added to `led_layout_json` (~15 lines) — the raw material for continuous-light rendering. (d) `apply_config` preserves `expected_fx` / `last_rgb_upload` for devices whose effect is byte-identical, plus startup index precompute from the persisted spec. SHIPPABLE ON ITS OWN: (a) alone makes the owner's two Strimers render a real preview in the EXISTING UI instead of a 'no layout map for this device' dead card, and (d) alone makes daemon restart and device rename cost zero flash writes.
3. M5b-1 — THE RENDERER, DROPPED INTO THE EXISTING SLOT. Replace StageCanvas's per-LED arc fills with the three-pass segment-continuous light renderer (unlit guides → half-res diffusion blit → gradient-stroked cores), plus the first-run benchmark and the three quality tiers. Land it at the CURRENT 280px canvas size inside the CURRENT Lighting screen — no layout work, no new components. Also split the rAF loop from the frame-buffer swap so a palette edit no longer tears down the ResizeObserver, re-enters `loadWasm()` and resets `frameIdx = 0` on every keystroke. Instant, dramatic visual upgrade with the smallest possible blast radius, and it de-risks the single biggest perf unknown in the whole design before any composition work is committed to.

M5b-2 — THE STORE REWRITE. `stageStore` goes from one global `spec` + `selectedMac` to `{ scope, devices: Map<mac, {committed, draft, phase}> }`, seeded from `config.devices[].effect` via the existing `specFromWire`. Until it lands, Strimers stay honestly captioned `wire order · not yet measured`.
12. M5d — THE FAST PATH AND THE SECOND SCREEN. Tauri global shortcut raising the window into the last-edited scope; a tray menu that commits Blackout / Night / the last three Looks without opening the window; `⌘1..9` to load a Look and `Enter` to commit, two keys from cold; the <150 ms config-only first paint that composes the app before WASM loads; and the detachable full-screen Rig View for the second monitor. Last because it is pure leverage on top of everything else, and first to cut if the owner does not want it.
